How the two components work

Seasonal (heavy) soiling builds up through the dry season — the rising curve. More dust each day means the loss accelerates; winter rain resets it to zero. This is the classic question: how far into the dry season before cleaning pays?

Persistent soiling is the part rain doesn't wash away — pollen films, cemented deposits, biological growth that carry over year to year. We catch it by comparing each month to the same month last year (adjusted for ~0.5%/yr normal panel degradation). Because it's always present, it lifts the whole curve so breakeven arrives sooner — sometimes on day one.

The flagging rule: in Year 1, flag when seasonal loss alone exceeds cleaning cost.

The math

Seasonal loss (the curve shape). On day N of a dry spell the panel is N × soiling_rate dirtier than on day 0, so each successive day loses a bit more. Summing day 1…N gives a triangular sum:

heavy_loss($) = daily_kWh × soil_rate × N(N+1)/2 × $/kWh

The N(N+1)/2 term is what bends the curve. At day 60 it's 1,830; at day 120 it's 7,260 — 4× larger, not 2×. Waiting twice as long quadruples the loss.

Persistent offset (the flat floor). This doesn't grow with dry days — it's already being lost year-round from carryover residue. We derive it by comparing this month's top-quartile 4-hour production block to the same month last year, then projecting that deficit across the full year:

R_persist($) = year_over_year_loss% × (kW × 1,750 kWh × $0.375/kWh)

For a 3% persistent loss on an 8 kW system that's roughly $157 — fixed, no matter how many dry days have passed.

Combined breakeven. In Year 2+, total recoverable value is the sum of both, and cleaning pays once that total × recovery% clears the cost:

(heavy_loss(N) + R_persist) × recovery% ≥ cleaning_cost

Solving for the breakeven day:

N ≈ √( (cleaning_cost / recovery% − R_persist) × 2 / (daily_kWh × soil_rate × rate) )

If the term inside the root is already negative, the persistent loss alone covers the cleaning cost and breakeven is day zero — the case this model cares most about: arrays worth flagging at the start of every season.

Two-consecutive-months rule. We evaluate at the end of May, June, and July. In Year 2+, the combined value must clear the cost in two consecutive windows before flagging — one weak month is weather noise; two in a row is a real pattern. The faint vertical lines on the chart mark those windows.

Seasonal curve uses Kimber (2007) linear accumulation. Persistent offset uses an Enphase-style flagging model: year-over-year loss vs. the same month prior year, adjusted for 0.5%/yr panel degradation, valued at a $0.375/kWh blended annual rate. Soiling rates: ~0.05%/day average, ~0.10%/day heavy (top quartile) per Mejía & Kleissl (2013). Recovery percentages from UCSD 2013 field-cleaning data. Assumes 5.5 peak-sun-hours/day.
